# Recorded contact sidecars

A contact sidecar is optional presentation data in `data/motion/contacts/<take-id>.json`. It uses schema version 1 and identifies the exact take by id, frame count, and frames per second. Intervals are inclusive take-frame coordinates, and activation and release are linear fade durations in frames. A zero duration gives a full-weight boundary; adjacent intervals may share a boundary only if at least one contact fades to zero at that boundary. Samples contain both zero-weight endpoints when fades are specified. The data never determines a match outcome.

Each annotation identifies an actor, recorded source joint and side, and a distinct contact point: wrist, palm, or sole. The `hand` joints in a take are wrist positions; a palm or sole requires the displayed body's corresponding offset. Its target is the other actor's named anatomical surface and side, or the mat. `origin` is `manual` for a manually observed annotation, `inferred` for an estimate, or `authored` for an intentionally designed presentation constraint. `evidence` describes the observation and its uncertainty, or the rationale for an authored constraint. Confidence is a stated 0–1 assessment; neither an origin nor a confidence value is a verification claim.

## Coordinates

Mat anchors are points in the original take's world coordinates, in metres and Y up. They must use the same placement, coordinate-system conversion, scale, pair-facing reflection and translation as the recorded joints. In particular, reflecting world X alone is incorrect when the pair faces another direction or has a nonzero origin.

Actor anchors use dimensionless canonical segment coordinates, not an articulation's transform-local metres. Their origin is the proximal segment endpoint; +Y points from proximal to distal. Construct +Z by projecting the actor's anatomical anterior direction onto the plane perpendicular to +Y and normalizing it. +X is the normalized cross product +Y × +Z, and recompute +Z as +X × +Y. Thus the frame is right-handed. X and Z are measured in units of the segment's corresponding lateral and anterior radius; Y is measured in units of segment length. A runtime reconstructs the point as `proximal + X * lateralRadius * axisX + Y * length * axisY + Z * anteriorRadius * axisZ`. Radii, length and endpoints are properties of the displayed body, so a point adapts to body size. The current physical body exposes one collision radius per segment; the presentation consumer uses it for both radial axes, approximating a circular cross section. If a required endpoint, anterior direction, length, or radius is unavailable or degenerate, the runtime must skip the constraint rather than fabricate an axis or location.

For limbs the proximal and distal endpoints follow the joint chain: shoulder–elbow, elbow–wrist, wrist–palm centre, hip–knee, knee–ankle, and ankle–toe. Torso uses pelvis–neck. Pelvis uses the midpoint of the actual upper-leg origins to the spine bone; neck uses neck bone to head bone. The head proxy runs from the head-bone origin to a top extrapolated as the bind-measured head centre plus its offset from the head-bone origin. The head centre follows the actual posed head bone. This is a rendered body proxy, not an external measurement. Neck and head both use the physical head collision radius; pelvis uses the physical hips radius. The anterior reference is the chest anterior for torso and upper limbs, the posed head anterior for the head, the neck segment anterior for the neck, and pelvis anterior for pelvis and lower limbs. The body representation must provide anatomical anterior and the two radii; a joint-only recording does not by itself verify these quantities. Side is required for paired surfaces and `centre` for midline surfaces.

Sampling with mirroring swaps source joint and side and target side; reversing actors swaps actor indices. It deliberately leaves anchor values unchanged. The playback placement transforms a mat point once. For an actor point, mirroring reconstructs the corresponding opposite-side frame and reflects its canonical X coordinate once; it must not additionally reflect the resulting point. The consumer is responsible for applying that reflection because sampling has no playback placement or body frame. PhysicalBody grip anchors are articulation-local metre coordinates and cannot be used as canonical coordinates without conversion.

The runtime palm anchor is the physical hand-volume centre and the sole anchor is the modeled foot-box bottom centre. These are collision-geometry proxies, not measured skin or shoe-surface contact points. Engine hand grips take priority over sidecar hand constraints. Constraint residuals and full target gaps are reported separately; applying a constraint is not evidence of an observed human contact.
